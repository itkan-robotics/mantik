### What is a Pose?

A **pose** is where the robot is and which way it faces. In FTC you often write it as **(x, y, θ)**: position on the field (inches or meters) and heading **θ** (radians or degrees). Path followers and odometry both reason in poses.

See: [gm0: Pose](https://gm0.org/en/latest/docs/software/concepts/odometry.html#pose)

### Coordinate Frames and Field Representation

The field uses a flat 2D frame. One corner is usually **(0, 0)**; heading is measured from the **x** axis. You will convert between **robot-centric** (forward/left on the robot) and **field-centric** (fixed directions on the field) when driving or planning.

See: [Road Runner: Coordinate Frame](https://acme-robotics.gitbook.io/road-runner/tour/coordinate-frame)

### Path Representations

A **path** is an ordered set of poses or waypoints the robot should follow. Segments can be straight lines, arcs, or smooth curves (splines, Bézier curves). Planners sample the path into reference poses over time for the follower to track.

### Why Poses and Paths Matter

Autonomous that only uses timed power is hard to repeat. Poses and paths let you plan scoring routes, avoid obstacles, and tune one routine when the field layout is fixed.
