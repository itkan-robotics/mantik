### Overview

PID covers most mechanisms on a typical robot. State-space models, LQR, and multi-input control matter when several states are coupled (swerve modules, multi-joint arms, balancing) or when you need coordinated control across more than one actuator.

<RulesBox title="Techniques" subtitle="Key concepts:">

- **State-Space:** Models systems using state variables (position, velocity) and matrix equations.
- **LQR (linear quadratic regulator):** Picks gains that trade tracking error against how hard you drive the motors.
- **Feedforward:** Predicts required output based on physics models (gravity, friction, inertia).
- **MIMO:** Multi-Input Multi-Output control for coupled systems (e.g., Swerve).

</RulesBox>

### State-Space Control

State-space control uses `ẋ = Ax + Bu` to model how position, velocity, and other states evolve together. You can command more than one related variable at once (for example arm angle and arm angular velocity). WPILib provides `LinearSystem` and `LinearSystemLoop`.

### LQR

LQR chooses feedback gains by minimizing a cost function. You set **Q** (how much you penalize state error) and **R** (how much you penalize control effort). Higher Q tends toward snappier tracking; higher R tends toward gentler motor commands.

### When to Use

**PID:** Intakes, elevators, single-axis arms, shooters, and most independent mechanisms.

**Advanced control:** Balancing robots, inverted pendulums, tightly coupled multi-DOF arms, or full drivetrain models where WPILib’s state-space tooling is already wired in.
